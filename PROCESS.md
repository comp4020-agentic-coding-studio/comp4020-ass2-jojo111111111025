# Process

## Course concept
I designed **One More Episode: The Psychology of Binge-Watching**, a university course examining why people continue watching, how streaming interfaces shape viewing behaviour, and how these mechanisms can be interrupted. I chose binge-watching because it connects psychological concepts with familiar digital product experiences. Rather than making a generic psychology website, I structured the course around a progression from individual viewing decisions to streaming interface mechanisms, broader viewing outcomes, and strategies for breaking the viewing loop.

## Investigation and architecture
I first translated the Assignment 2 brief into checkable requirements and built corresponding tests ([`805d06f`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-jojo111111111025/commit/805d06f)). I then used the Agent to inspect the starter project and help propose the information architecture, content model, and interaction strategy ([`20ae414`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-jojo111111111025/commit/20ae414)). However, I made the course structure and teaching decisions: the site needed twelve weeks, a different experiment for each week, assessments, policies, people, and lecture slides.

During content-model development, I initially explored making some lecture narrative fields mandatory, but found that this interfered with the Astro build. I therefore changed the approach and checked curriculum completeness through the built course index ([`5b9f548`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-jojo111111111025/commit/5b9f548)). This preserved the content requirement without making development unnecessarily brittle.

## Prototyping and iteration
I first developed Week 1 as a prototype, connecting the "one more episode" effect with an interactive experiment ([`68308d3`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-jojo111111111025/commit/68308d3)). I then developed a different interaction for Week 2 ([`ba8e792`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-jojo111111111025/commit/ba8e792)) and separately developed the Week 6 time-perception experiment ([`3f36726`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-jojo111111111025/commit/3f36726)). These prototypes established that each week should have its own interaction connected to its psychological question rather than repeatedly using the same widget.

The Agent mainly acted as an implementation partner: it helped inspect existing code, implement and modify components, write interaction logic and course content, and run project checks. I remained responsible for teaching content, psychological questions, relationships between interactions and course concepts, and final content judgement. This also responded to my Assignment 1 feedback: the marker noted that the interaction communicated information, but the surrounding text and imagery could be more polished. For Assignment 2, I therefore treated text, information structure, page consistency, and overall course coherence as part of the design.

## Scaling and refinement
After establishing the prototype direction, I completed different interactions for Weeks 3–12 ([`69bcd9d`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-jojo111111111025/commit/69bcd9d)) and added twelve lecture decks, weekly quizzes, Assignment 1, the Final Project, recording placeholders, and supporting course pages.

I did not treat the Agent's generated output as the final version. I spent time checking page details and actual functionality, then asked the Agent to make targeted changes. I checked page titles and course names, lecture and assessment card alignment, weekly schedule links, recording entries, and starter/template content across People, Policies, Sessions, and assessment pages. I also checked desktop and mobile presentation. When problems were found, I asked the Agent to make focused content, structural, or CSS changes rather than redesigning the whole site.

The course structure deliberately distinguishes levels of the topic: Weeks 1–6 introduce mechanisms, Weeks 7–10 examine broader binge-watching patterns and trade-offs, Week 11 focuses on interruption, and Week 12 synthesises the course. The Final Project therefore asks students to connect multiple mechanisms into a complete chain.
