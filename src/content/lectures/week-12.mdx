## This week's question

Every week so far isolated one mechanism at a time on purpose — a
cliffhanger with everything else held constant, an autoplay countdown with
nothing else varying, a reward schedule tested on its own. That isolation
was necessary to see each mechanism clearly. It was also artificial: a real
viewing session never runs one mechanism in isolation. This final week asks
the question that isolating each one, week by week, was always building
toward: **what happens once the mechanisms we've studied stop taking turns
and start running together?**

## A session is a chain, not a list

Read back over the course and a pattern appears that no single week could
show on its own: each mechanism's effect tends to land right where the next
mechanism's starting condition needs it to. A cliffhanger (Week 3) leaves a
question open. Autoplay (Week 4) answers that openness before a decision has
a chance to happen — it doesn't have to be persuasive, because the pause
where persuasion would be needed simply isn't there. What plays next
sometimes pays off and sometimes doesn't (Week 5), which is exactly the
condition that keeps the next check feeling worth making. What gets
recommended after that narrows toward more of whatever just worked (Week
8). A social cue (Week 9) can nudge which of two narrowed options gets
picked, for free. And every episode in the chain costs the same real thing
— time against a wake-up time that isn't moving (Week 10) — a cost that's
easy to discount because, at every single link in this chain, something
more immediate is happening on screen right now.

None of these mechanisms needs any of the others to work. But none of them
was studied here as if it were the whole story, either. They chain.

## A familiar scene

Nobody sits down and consciously invokes six psychological mechanisms in
order. It just happens: an ending doesn't resolve, the next thing is already
counting down, most episodes are fine but one is unexpectedly great, the
next row of tiles looks tailored, a friend's name is attached to one of
them, and somewhere in there a wake-up time quietly stopped being part of
the decision. Naming each piece afterward doesn't make any single piece
more powerful. It does make the whole chain visible as a chain, instead of
as one continuous, undifferentiated pull that's hard to reason about because
it doesn't seem to have any parts.

## What just happened?

This wasn't a new mechanism, and the score at the end isn't the point — the
chain is. Each scene above used a mechanism from an earlier week, kept
exactly as that week defined it. What's different from every earlier week's
demo is that this time they're shown as links in one session instead of six
separate experiments, because that's closer to how any real session actually
runs.

**What this demonstration can't tell you:** six fixed scenes and one
made-up connective narrative are a teaching model, not a study of how these
mechanisms actually interact in a real product, and however many you
identified correctly says nothing diagnostic about you. The genuine, open
question this page can't answer is an empirical one: whether these
mechanisms combine additively, whether some pairs reinforce each other more
than others, or whether one dominates the rest — that needs real research,
not a six-scene demo built for a course website.

## Reflection, and a look back

Across twelve weeks, this course asked one question from a dozen different
angles: why does "one more" so rarely feel like the decision it actually is?
Every mechanism named along the way turned out to share the same shape —
not a flaw in anyone's character, but a property of an interface built to
make continuing easier than stopping, one small design choice at a time.
Think back to whichever week's demonstration landed hardest for you. Now
that you've seen it as one link in a longer chain rather than a standalone
effect: does it change how you'd choose to interrupt a real version of that
chain — and if so, where in it?

If you want to trace how any of this was built, Week 1 — "The One More
Episode Effect" — is where the course started, and every episode between
here and there is still exactly where it was.
