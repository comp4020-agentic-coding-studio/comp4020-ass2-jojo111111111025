## The brief

> Take the mechanisms this course named one at a time, week by week, and show
> how they chain together into a single real session or subscription — not
> as twelve separate points, but as one connected argument.

Every week from 1 to 12 isolated one mechanism on purpose, holding everything
else constant so it could be seen clearly. [Week 12's own
lecture](/lectures/week-12/) makes the point this project asks you to run
with: a real viewing session never runs one mechanism at a time — one
mechanism's effect tends to land right where the next mechanism's starting
condition needs it to. Pick a real or realistic streaming session or
subscription history and trace that chain through it: which weeks'
mechanisms are operating, in what order, and how does an earlier one set up
the conditions the next one needs?

Then go one step further than description. [Week 11](/lectures/week-11/)
showed that the same interruption placed at different points in a cycle
produces very different outcomes. Using that reasoning, name one specific
point in the chain you traced where a small interruption would do the most
good, and explain why that point beats the others you could have picked.

A strong response does what the course's own final week does: it treats the
semester as a chain, not a list. The judgement being tested is whether you
can show one mechanism's output becoming the next mechanism's input in a
concrete case — not just correctly recall twelve definitions in one
document.

## Weeks covered

The final project draws on the full semester — Weeks 1 through 12, every
mechanism this course has named. It is released in Week 9 and due in Week
12, but it isn't limited to the second half of the course: it is
specifically the piece of work that asks you to look back across everything,
including the material from weeks already assessed in [Assignment
1](/assessments/assignment-1/).

## What you submit

- **The artefact** — an annotated timeline or diagram of a real or
  realistic streaming session or subscription history, marking where each
  mechanism you're tracing activates and labelling it with the week it came
  from.
- **A written analysis** — walking the chain stage by stage, showing how
  each mechanism's effect creates the starting condition the next one needs,
  the way Week 12's lecture does across a handful of examples.
- **A closing intervention** — naming one specific point in your chain
  where a Week-11-style interruption would have the biggest effect, and
  explaining why, using that week's own reasoning about placement rather
  than a general appeal to willpower.
