# The One More Episode Effect

Why does stopping feel so much harder than continuing?

---

## A familiar situation

<FlowRow steps={["Episode ends", '"Next episode" appears', '"Just one more"']} loop />

Late at night, this sequence can repeat far longer than planned — without
ever feeling like a single big decision.

---

## Why stopping is difficult

<FactorCards items={[
  { label: "Unresolved narrative", description: "A cliffhanger leaves something specific still open" },
  { label: "Immediate reward", description: "The next episode is a reward that's ready right now" },
  { label: "Reduced stopping points", description: "There's no natural pause built into the handoff" },
  { label: "Low effort to continue", description: "Continuing takes no action; stopping takes one" },
]} />

None of these has to change your opinion of the show to change what you do next.

---

## The streaming loop

<FlowRow steps={["Trigger", "Anticipation", "Episode", "Reward", "Next episode"]} loop />

The stopping decision gets harder right at the handoff from **reward** to
**next episode** — the moment with the least friction and the most pull.

---

## The One More Episode Experiment

You're about to run a small experiment on yourself: the same "stop or
continue" choice, presented three different ways.

- **Baseline** — both options equally easy
- **Friction** — the "stop" option is delayed by a few seconds
- **Anticipation** — "continue" comes with a specific unresolved detail

Watch what changes about the decision itself, not just what you choose.

## Takeaway

Stopping isn't just a yes-or-no willpower question — the environment around
the decision changes how easy it feels to make. The experiment you're about
to run lets you examine that for yourself, on yourself.
