# Breaking the Loop

Where can a small intervention actually break a cycle that keeps itself going?

---

## A familiar scene

"I'll stop after this one" gets said at the *reward* stage — right when a
resolution has just landed and a new recommendation is already glowing on
screen — instead of at the *autoplay* stage, before the next episode has
even started counting down.

---

## This week's question

Ten weeks in, the pattern is obvious: these mechanisms don't just nudge one
decision each — strung together, they form a loop. **Where** in that cycle
does an interruption actually change what happens next?

---

## The five-stage loop

<FlowRow steps={["Cue", "Episode", "Autoplay", "Reward", "Recommendation"]} loop />

The recommendation is itself the next cue. Nothing requires four laps, or
forty — it just keeps going until something interrupts it.

## The interactive experiment: Break the Loop

A small, deterministic version of the loop, run three ways: no intervention,
an early intervention, and a late one. The intervention is identical each
time — only its position changes.

## Takeaway

A person who "finally stopped" after five episodes used exactly the same
tool as a person who stopped after one. The difference wasn't willpower —
it was where the interruption landed in an already-turning cycle.
